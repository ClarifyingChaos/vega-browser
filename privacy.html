Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

Vega is a web browser for the Mac. This page says what Vega does with your data.

On your Mac

Your browsing history, tabs, bookmarks, passwords and website data stay on your Mac. Vega has no ads and no analytics, and does not sell or share your data.

Sign in with Google (optional)

Signing in with Google is only used to sync Vega between your own Macs. Vega asks Google for:

  • Your email address (openid, email), to show which account you are signed in with and to keep your sync files apart from everyone else's.
  • Vega's own hidden app folder in your Google Drive (drive.appdata). Vega cannot see or change any other file in your Drive.

Your Google sign-in token is kept in your Mac's Keychain. You can sign out at any time in Vega's Settings › Account, and remove Vega's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Vega Sync

When you turn on sync, Vega stores your spaces, favorites, pinned and open tabs, and settings with the Vega Sync server (run on Cloudflare), in iCloud Drive, or in Vega's Google Drive app folder. Passwords are only synced when you set a sync passphrase, and are encrypted on your Mac with it before they leave; your other synced data is encrypted the same way once a passphrase is set. The server keeps one set of files per Google account and does not read them. Turning sync off, or removing a Mac from the chain, stops sending data.

Crash reports (optional)

If you turn on crash reports in Settings › Updates, a report about the crash is sent so it can be fixed. It is off unless you turn it on.
